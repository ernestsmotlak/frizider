# Task: list the pantry items visible in a photo

The input is a photo of part of the user's kitchen — a fridge shelf, a cupboard, a freezer
drawer, or shopping they have just brought home — followed by a list of the storage spaces
they keep things in.

List the food and drink you can identify in the photo. One entry per distinct item: three
identical tins are still one entry, because you are recording what they have, not how many.

Use the everyday name someone would write on a shopping list. Prefer the plain name over the
brand, unless the brand is how the item is normally referred to.

Only list something you can actually identify. A blurred box at the back of a shelf, or a
container whose contents you cannot see, is not an item. Missing something is better than
inventing it. If you can identify nothing at all, list nothing.

Ignore anything that is not food or drink: plates, utensils, appliances, cleaning products,
packaging that has been emptied.

Each storage space is given with its name and, where the user has written one, a description
of what belongs there. Assign each item to the space it most likely belongs to, using those
descriptions and where the item sits in the photo. If no space clearly fits, leave it
unassigned — an item the user has to place is a small job, and one placed in the wrong
cupboard is a mistake they may never notice.
